Screen clock
Live screen clock
Show a clock that ticks every minute. Off, the screen shows the time of the last reading instead — and this is the single biggest choice on this page: the device then wakes 288 times a day instead of 1056, and lasts about half again as long. Everything it measures is unchanged.
Ultra Save always shows the last-update time instead of a live clock — that is most of what makes it Ultra Save. Pick another mode to turn the clock back on.
Night mode
Quiet hours
At night the CO₂ sensor measures less often and the device stops going online, to save battery. The screen keeps showing live readings — it costs almost nothing to update and a bedroom at night is what this device is for. Serious air alerts still get through; the gentler ones wait for the morning.

Needs the clock set — the device syncs time over Wi-Fi. Uses your timezone from Settings.

Connectivity

Choose how the device talks to your home. One mode at a time.

Smart home (Matter)

The device joins your smart home over Thread and works there on its own: CO₂, temperature, humidity and air quality appear in the app, and the screen keeps showing everything it measures.

Paused in this mode: outdoor weather and forecast, Telegram and MQTT — Thread gives the device no way out to the internet. Hold the button for 3 seconds at any time to open this page again: over your Wi-Fi if it knows one (no QR code), otherwise over its own. On your Wi-Fi it also sets its clock, and a waiting update installs when the page closes.

You need a Thread border router, for example a Google Nest Hub (2nd gen) or Nest Wifi Pro, an Apple HomePod mini, HomePod (2nd gen) or Apple TV 4K (Wi-Fi + Ethernet), an Amazon Echo (4th gen), or a SmartThings Station.

    Connect

    Smart Meteo uses 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Make sure your hotspot allows it.

    In smart-home mode this network is optional. When it is saved, holding the button opens this page on your network (no QR code), and updates install over it.

    Passwords are stored only on the device and never shown back in full.

    MQTT broker

    The device publishes one retained JSON message to <topic>/state once per radio window, then disconnects and sleeps — it does not hold a session open. Alerts go to the hub; Telegram can send them as well if you want both — that is in Settings.

    Encrypt the connection
    Required for a broker outside your home network: the device refuses to connect to one without it rather than sending your readings in the clear. On your own network it is optional — but off, the broker password and every reading cross your Wi-Fi unencrypted, readable by anything else on it. Leave it on unless your broker cannot do TLS.

    Home Assistant: one automation does the rest (recommended)

    The readings appear in Home Assistant by themselves. This one automation adds the three things that cannot announce themselves:

    • Outdoor weather to the device, from the weather your Home Assistant already has. The device then stops fetching its own — no API key, no internet, and no extra time with the radio on.
    • Alerts to your phones — every phone with the Home Assistant app, found by itself, plus a notification inside Home Assistant.
    • A warning after a day of silence — a flat battery or a lost network otherwise looks like nothing at all.
    1. In Home Assistant: Settings → Automations & scenes → Create automation → Create new automation.
    2. Three dots, top right → Edit in YAML. Select everything there and paste this over it.
    3. Save. Nothing to fill in — it was written for this device, from the settings on this page.
    
          
    It refuses to publish while the weather is unavailable, and never sends an
          empty forecast: a gap in the data should look like a gap, not like a reading. Units are
          converted, so °F and mph work the same.
    
          
    Needs Home Assistant 2024.10 or newer and a weather integration — a new Home
          Assistant sets up Met.no by itself. Phone alerts use the notify entity of the current
          Home Assistant app; with an older app they still appear under Notifications. Changed the topic
          prefix, the name or the city here? Copy it again — they are written into it. Several devices:
          one automation each, each with its own topic prefix.
    
          
    
            
            Nothing breaks if you skip this. After six hours without a weather message the
            device fetches its own again, exactly as it does in Wi-Fi mode — that only needs a city in
            Settings.
    
        
    

        
        
    
          Another hub: what the device reads and sends
          
    Weather in: a retained JSON message on
          smartmeteo/weather. Retained matters: the device is asleep
          almost all the time, so the message has to be waiting for it rather than sent to it.
    
          
    {
  "temp":  14.2,            // °C, outdoor now
  "feels": 12.8,            // °C, apparent
  "hum":   64,              // %
  "wind":  3.1,             // m/s
  "desc":  "Partly cloudy", // shown as words, keep it short
  "icon":  "02d",           // OpenWeatherMap-style code, picks the drawing
  "city":  "Darmstadt",     // name shown above the block
  "tmin":  9.1,             // °C, today's forecast low
  "tmax":  17.4,            // °C, today's forecast high
  "forecast": [             // the days AFTER today, earliest first, up to 4
    {"date": "2026-09-12", "icon": "10d", "tmin": 9, "tmax": 16}
  ]
}
    
          
    Every field is optional except temp; anything missing keeps its
          previous value. tmin/tmax only ever widen today's range — the
          device keeps the day's real low and high itself, because a forecast only describes the hours
          still to come. forecast fills the four-day row: each day needs all four fields,
          date is a local calendar date, and days not after today are ignored. A list
          replaces the previous one whole, and an empty list clears the row — so leave the key out
          when there are no days. Icon codes follow OpenWeatherMap: 01d/01n clear,
          02d partly cloudy, 03/04 cloud, 09 showers,
          10d/10n rain, 11 thunder, 13 snow,
          50 fog.
    
          
    Alerts out: one message per event on
          smartmeteo/alert, not retained —
          {"device", "level", "title", "text"}, where level is
          advisory, warning or clear (back to normal) and the
          first line of text is a ready headline. The same alert also rides in
          <topic>/state for as long as it stands: show it from there, send it from
          here.

    CO₂ calibration
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Fresh-air calibration
    
        iThe CO₂ sensor sleeps between readings to save the battery, and in that mode it cannot calibrate itself. Use this when readings look wrong. Temperature has its own calibration below.
      
    
      
    Put the device outside — a balcony is enough — and press the button. It first waits until the readings stop falling, then takes six more: usually 11 to 15 minutes, never more than 26. The device's own screen shows how far it has got and when it is done. Then bring it back to its place. The device cannot tell where it is standing, so it asks you once before it starts and then believes the answer.
    
      
    Live CO₂ readingiUpdated once a minute while this page is open. Outdoors it settles near 420 ppm.—
    
      
      
    
      
    
        
      
    
      
    You can close this page while it runs: the device finishes on its own and sends the result to Telegram, if the bot is set up. The baseline is always anchored to 420 ppm, fresh outdoor air — the value cannot be edited, because a wrong one would throw off every future reading.

    Match a thermometer
    
        iThe sensor subtracts a fixed amount from its reading to allow for the heat of the electronics around it. This sets that amount from a thermometer you trust.

    1. Put a thermometer you trust next to the device and give both a few minutes to settle.
      2. 
        
    2. Type what it says and press Calibrate. The correction applies from the next measurement.
      3.

    Device password
    
      
    One password opens this console, the device's own setup Wi-Fi and its QR code.
      It is printed on the device's screen while this page is open, so it cannot be lost — and a
      factory reset draws a new one.
    
      
    
        
        
        
    Exactly 8 characters. Letters of either case, digits and symbols —

        e-ink screen for you to read, and the widest character it can draw fits eight times on that
        line. The two it cannot take are the ones that do not fit or cannot be seen there.

    Air-quality details
    
        iExtra readings from the BME690 gas sensor. They become reliable once calibration reaches 2–3 / 3.
      
    
      
    Readings behind the air verdict, and the two sensors' own climate side by side.
    
      
    Static IAQiBosch: “unscaled indoor-air-quality estimate”. It starts at 0 and has no upper limit. Bosch’s 0–500 scale and its classes belong to the main IAQ index, not to this value, so nothing is judged on it.—
    
      
    Gas resistance—
    
      
    Gas signaliRelative gas-sensor reading. It RISES with gases/VOCs: ~0% is the cleanest this sensor has seen here, 100% is the most. It's relative, not a concentration — use the verdict and IAQ for the overall picture.—
    
      
    Climate: SCD41iThe room temperature and humidity shown everywhere else. The SCD41 has no heater beside its sensing element, so it reads the room rather than itself.—
    
      
    Climate: BME690iThe same air as measured by the gas sensor. Its 320 °C gas heater sits next to the element, so it reads warmer and drier than the room. Used only inside the gas algorithm — never displayed as the room climate.—
    
      
    Difference—
    
      
    Pressure at sea leveliWhat the barometric forecast judges the LEVEL by — the same fall means a weakening high at 1023 hPa and an arriving low at 1008, and only the level tells them apart. The device reduces its own reading using its altitude, which it measures from a weather report, or takes from the chosen city. Dashes mean it knows neither, and then it shows the pressure without a forecast word.—
    
      
    Coldest walliHumidity at the coldest surface in the room — what the Damp verdict is actually decided on, since mould grows on walls and not in air. Estimated from the outdoor temperature for a building that meets code (DIN 4108-2, f=0.70); an older cold corner runs colder still. Condensation and mould risk. Damp warns above 80 % here and clears below 70 %, where nothing grows at all. Dashes mean there is no current outdoor reading, and then the room humidity alone is used — above 70 %, clearing below 62 %.—
